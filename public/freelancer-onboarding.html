1 · The Why

Breathe Collection is an app that teaches people the breathwork techniques that actually move the nervous system — box breathing, the physiological sigh, Wim Hof, slow nasal coherence. Most people only meet these methods once, in a stressful moment, on a noisy feed. We want them to meet them well.

Your role is the front door. The videos you make are the first thing a panic-attack-sufferer or burnt-out founder sees before they ever open the app. They're not ads. They're invitations.

"If a video doesn't make me exhale, it isn't ready."

3 · How the Studio Works

The dashboard is intentionally bare. One text box, placeholder reads "Next". You drop a single idea — a feeling, a moment, a body sensation — and the pipeline produces a 15-second loop ready for TikTok and Instagram.

  • Brief or upload. Write a one-sentence idea — or drop your own photo in. Both paths work.
  • Generate. The system produces one image (or uses yours) and animates it into a seamless 15s loop.
  • Review. You watch, you approve, you nudge. We never ship something neither of us would exhale to.
  • Hand off. Finished loops land in Dropbox — you grab, you post, you tag the right techniques.

5 · Guardrails

  • 15-second loop. Seamless. 9:16. 1080×1920.
  • One image, animated. Slow, deep, atmospheric — the arc lives in the motion, not in cuts.
  • No boomerang humans. A person mid-breath played in reverse creates a phantom inhale. Forward-only animation, always.
  • No baked-in text, logos, or branding. Captions live in TikTok, not in the composite.
  • No metronomic orb. Particles or fill carry the rhythm; an orb-as-clock fights the body motion.
  • Motion judged by MediaPipe, not Gemini. Deterministic, not aesthetic vibes.

6 · How to Write a Brief

A good brief is one sentence and three nouns. Lead with the feeling. Name the setting in three words. Name the body.

The slow exhale that drops the shoulders at the end of a long Tuesday. Alpine fjord, gold hour. Woman, eyes closed, slow exhale.
